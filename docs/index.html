Questions people actually ask

If yours isn't here, email it — you'll get a straight answer, not a sales call.

What does automated quote follow-up actually do?

Every quote you send gets followed up automatically on day 2, 5 and 10 — politely, from your own email address, in the same thread the customer is already looking at. They answer with one click: interested, not right now, or already booked. The moment they answer, the chasing stops.

No recovery percentage is quoted here, because it depends entirely on how many quotes you send and what they are worth. The arithmetic is easier to check than a statistic. The service is $149/month. Work out what one average job is worth to you: at $600 a job, it pays for itself if it recovers one job every four months. Count the quotes you sent last month that nobody chased, and you will know quickly whether it is worth trying.

What does the Review Engine do, and isn't filtering reviews against the rules?

After every finished job the customer gets one short email asking how it went, with five stars to tap. If they tap 4 or 5, the next screen is your Google review page. If they tap 1, 2 or 3, the next screen is a private feedback box, and you get an email within the minute telling you who to call.

On the rules — this matters, so here is the straight answer. What Google prohibits, and what the FTC's 2024 reviews rule targets, is gating: only asking happy customers, or stopping unhappy ones from posting. This does neither. Every customer is asked the same question in the same email, and after an unhappy customer has had their say, the public review link is still on the page. Nobody is filtered and nobody is blocked.

What it actually buys you is timing. An unhappy customer who is heard the same day usually talks to you instead of to the internet. That is worth more than the reviews you gain, because a public one-star costs you the next several enquiries.

Quote Follow-Up Recovery and the Review Engine are both typically live in about three days. Nothing is waiting on approval, because both run on email rather than the phone network. Custom builds run 2–4 weeks depending on scope.

Do I need to change how I send quotes?

No. Keep quoting exactly the way you do now — your own template, your own prices, your own software. You add the quote in about twenty seconds and the follow-up runs from there. Nothing the customer receives from you changes.

Nothing is stored on any server. The tools run in your browser, and anything they keep — an API key, a saved build — stays in that browser's local storage on your own device. No accounts, no tracking pixels, no analytics.
