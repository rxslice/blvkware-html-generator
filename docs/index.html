The old model

You buy the tool.

  • You learn the tool.
  • You hire people to operate the tool.
  • You connect it to five other tools.
  • You pay for it every month whether it accomplishes anything or not.

“I'll build you software.” → a number nobody can defend → months of development → you operate it.

The BlvkWare model

You define the job.

  • The agent is designed around it.
  • You get every file it needs, written for your business.
  • You build it with what you already use.
  • It starts at Draft, and you can see everything it did.

“What job do you need done?” → the agent, specified → you build it → it does the job.

Software isn't dead. Software you have to operate is. Traditional software gives your employees tools. The next generation of businesses won't just use software: their software will work for them.

Your business doesn't run in one app. Neither should your agent. Your quotes are in one place, your calendar in another, your invoices in a third, and the job that is actually costing you money runs across all of them. Every one of those will eventually have its own assistant, and each will be helpful inside its own walls. None of them will chase a quote through your inbox, your calendar and your accounting on their own, because none of them can see the other two. That gap is the whole job.

What a kit is

The complete specification of one agent: its instructions, the tools it may call and what each one may change, a workflow per capability, the records it keeps, the rules that hold it back, a setup checklist and its acceptance tests.

Every file is plain JSON or Markdown, written for the tools you already use.

What it is not

Running software. It does not connect to your systems by itself and holds no credentials. You, or whoever builds for you, load it into OpenAI or Anthropic models with tool calling, any MCP client, or n8n, Make or Zapier.

What running it costs, like model usage or a phone number, is between you and those providers.

How you buy it

Design it on the kit page, buy on Gumroad, and paste the licence key from your receipt to download. Your design is kept in your browser while you pay.

What is an AI agent, in plain terms, and how is it different from the automation I already have?

Automation follows a rule you wrote: if this happens, do that. It cannot handle anything you did not anticipate, which is why most automations quietly stop being used. The exceptions pile up and a person ends up doing the work anyway.

An agent reasons through the task instead of replaying a script. It reads what actually arrived, decides what the right next step is, uses your existing software to take it, and knows when a situation is outside what it should decide alone. When a customer replies with something the script never covered, the automation breaks and the agent handles it.

The practical test: automation gives somebody a faster tool and they still do the job. An agent does the job, and brings a person the part that needs one.

That last part matters and it is worth being plain about. Most of these do not empty a seat. They take the seventy or eighty per cent of a workload that is repetitive and leave the judgment, the relationships and the awkward exceptions with the person who was already doing them, who now spends their week on the part that actually needed them. If you are looking to remove a role rather than remove the work, say so and I will tell you honestly whether that is realistic for the job you have in mind. Usually it is not, and pretending otherwise is how these projects end badly.

What does it cost, and are there hidden fees?

$79 for a kit for an agent that owns one job, and $199 for one that runs an entire function. The size of what you design decides which, and the kit page says why. It is a one-off purchase, not a subscription, and you can change the design and download it again, up to 20 times.

What it costs to run the agent you build, such as model usage or a phone number, is between you and those providers. The kit never adds a fee of its own.

Is the kit a working agent?

No, and the difference matters. A kit is the complete specification of one agent: what it does, step by step, the tools it may call and what each one is allowed to change, the records it keeps, the rules that stop it acting beyond its authority, and the tests that prove it works. It does not connect to your systems by itself and holds no credentials.

You, or whoever builds for you, load it into the tools you already use: OpenAI or Anthropic models with tool calling, any MCP client, or an automation platform like n8n, Make or Zapier. Every file is plain JSON or Markdown, so nothing locks you to one vendor.

What stops the agent doing something it shouldn't?

The kit is written so the agent starts at Draft, where nothing that would reach one of your customers (an email, a text, a booking, a request for payment) happens without a person on your side sending it. It still keeps your own records straight while it works, because none of your customers sees that part.

Every tool in the kit carries its effect: read, internal or external. The autonomy policy says which effects each level allows, so whoever builds it can enforce the rules in code, not only in the prompt. Every action is logged before it happens, opt-outs are permanent, nothing is sent in quiet hours, and instructions that arrive inside a customer's message are treated as information, never as orders.

How it behaves once built is under your control, which is why the kit keeps it at Draft until its acceptance tests pass. How agents are controlled covers credentials, logs and the stop in full.

Do I have to replace the software I already use?

No, and you shouldn't. The agent operates the tools you already run: your CRM, your calendar, your inbox, your invoicing, your scheduling software. That is the whole idea: your team already has tools, what it doesn't have is someone to work them.

Tick the systems you use when you design the agent, and the kit's tools and integration notes are written for exactly those.

How quickly can this be running?

The kit downloads the moment you enter your licence key. How long the build takes is up to you and whoever builds it: the kit hands them the design, the tools, the workflows and the tests, which is most of the thinking.

One exception, stated up front: texting customers from a US business number needs A2P 10DLC carrier registration, which realistically takes weeks, including a manual review nobody can hurry. The kit flags it where it applies. Email and web channels have no such gate, which is why the first agent in most businesses runs on those.

What if it isn't what I needed?

Look before you buy: the kit page shows the full file list and a summary of everything in it before you pay. After buying, the money-back guarantee on the Gumroad product page applies, and a refunded key stops unlocking downloads.

Can I change it after I've bought it?

Yes. One key is one agent: add or remove capabilities, change its systems or your rules, and download it again as often as you need. The only thing that changes the price is the agent growing from one job into a whole function, and the kit page says so before it happens.

Every file is also yours to edit by hand. That is the point of plain JSON and Markdown.

Are the free tools actually free, and what's the catch?

They're genuinely free with no sign-up, no account and no payment. All three run entirely in your browser.

The catch, such as it is: they're the portfolio. They exist so you can judge the standard of the work before you spend anything, rather than reading claims about it.

What happens to my data when I use the tools?

Nothing is stored on any server. The tools run in your browser, and anything they keep (an API key, a saved build) stays in that browser's local storage on your own device. No accounts, no tracking pixels, no analytics.

Full detail is in the privacy policy.
