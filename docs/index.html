The old model

You buy the tool.

  • You learn the tool.
  • You hire people to operate the tool.
  • You connect it to five other tools.
  • You pay for it every month whether it accomplishes anything or not.

“I'll build you software.” → a number nobody can defend → months of development → you operate it.

The BlvkWare model

You define the job.

  • The agent is built around it, at a flat price.
  • The agent performs the job.
  • BlvkWare keeps it running.
  • You pay for a worker, and you can see what it did.

“What job do you need done?” → a fixed price on the page → the agent works → it stays working.

Software isn't dead. Software you have to operate is. Traditional software gives your employees tools. The next generation of businesses won't just use software — their software will work for them.

What is an AI agent, in plain terms, and how is it different from the automation I already have?

Automation follows a rule you wrote: if this happens, do that. It cannot handle anything you did not anticipate, which is why most automations quietly stop being used — the exceptions pile up and a person ends up doing the work anyway.

An agent reasons through the task instead of replaying a script. It reads what actually arrived, decides what the right next step is, uses your existing software to take it, and knows when a situation is outside what it should decide alone. When a customer replies with something the script never covered, the automation breaks and the agent handles it.

The practical test: automation gives your employees a faster tool. An agent means there is no longer a person in that seat at all.

What does it cost, and are there hidden fees?

An Operator — an agent that owns one job — is $3,500 to build and $349/month to keep employed. A Deputy, which owns an entire function, is $9,500 and $899/month. Pay the monthly annually and you get twelve months for the price of ten. A Custom Build, where what you need is software rather than a worker, starts at $2,500 with no monthly fee.

Components have published flat prices too, from $450 to $2,500, on the catalog page. There is no hourly rate anywhere in this and no estimate to wait for. The only variable line is volume: an Operator includes 2,000 agent actions a month and a Deputy 10,000, and beyond that it is $75 per additional thousand. Phone and SMS costs pass through at what the carrier charges, itemised, with nothing added on top.

Why is there a monthly fee — didn't I just buy the thing?

You didn't buy a thing. You hired a worker, and this is what keeps it employed.

An agent runs on models that cost money every time it thinks, it operates systems whose vendors change them without warning, and it makes decisions that need watching and correcting as your business changes. Agent Operations covers hosting, model costs, monitoring, unlimited tuning of its logic and its language, repairs when an integration breaks, business-hours support, a monthly performance report and a quarterly review.

Software you have to operate has a maintenance fee. This has a payroll. If it ever stops being worth it, cancel with thirty days' notice — there is no exit fee.

What happens when the agent gets something wrong?

Every agent goes live at Draft, where it cannot act on its own at all — it prepares the work and a person sends it. Nothing reaches one of your customers without someone seeing it. It only acts unsupervised at a level you turn on in writing, after you have watched it work, and you can turn it back down at any time.

Every action it takes is logged with what it saw, what it decided, why, and what it did — and actions are reversible. Before it ever touches a live customer it rehearses against your real history taking no action, and you read that log first.

The commercial answer is in the terms and it is not hidden: liability is capped at the fees you have paid. Any provider telling you their agent cannot make a mistake is either lying or has not shipped one.

Do I have to replace the software I already use?

No, and you shouldn't. The agent operates the tools you already run — your CRM, your calendar, your inbox, your invoicing, your scheduling software. That is the whole idea: your team already has tools, what it doesn't have is someone to work them.

Ripping out a working system to gain one feature is nearly always a bad trade. If something you run genuinely has no way in, that is worth knowing on day one rather than day nine, so list what you use when you send the order.

What if I stop paying — do I lose everything?

No. The agent pauses and your data is exported to you within five business days, all of it, in a format you can actually use.

If you would rather keep running it yourself, the source is available at twelve times the monthly fee. That is published here on purpose, because "you'll never get your business back out" is the fair objection to every managed service and it deserves an answer in writing rather than a reassurance on a call.

How quickly can this be running?

An Operator is live in ten business days and a Deputy in twenty-five. The sequence is the same every time: a signed job description on day one, wiring on days two and three, a rehearsal against your real history on day four, and it starts working on day five under supervision.

One exception, stated up front: anything involving a phone number or SMS in the United States needs A2P 10DLC carrier registration, which realistically takes three to six weeks including a manual review nobody can hurry. Email and web channels have no such gate, which is why the first agent in most businesses runs on those.

What if it doesn't work?

For an Operator: thirty days. If it doesn't do the job it was hired for, the build fee comes back.

For a Deputy the guarantee is stronger in the way that matters more. The acceptance criteria are written into the job description before any work starts, and the work continues at no additional charge until they are met. That is deliberately not a refund — a refund lets a supplier walk away from a hard build. This one doesn't let me.

Can I add things later, or does that mean a new project?

You can add anything on the component list at the same published price, years later if you like. No renegotiation, no new project, no new estimate. Adding a channel or another system to a working agent usually takes a few days.

The one rule that keeps flat pricing honest is written down so it never becomes an argument: changes after the job description is signed are components at list price, not free scope.

Are the free tools actually free, and what's the catch?

They're genuinely free with no sign-up, no account and no payment. AUGUR, SCRY and SIGIL run entirely in your browser.

The catch, such as it is: they're the portfolio. They exist so you can judge the standard of the work before you spend anything, rather than reading claims about it.

What happens to my data when I use the tools?

Nothing is stored on any server. The tools run in your browser, and anything they keep — an API key, a saved build — stays in that browser's local storage on your own device. No accounts, no tracking pixels, no analytics.

Full detail is in the privacy policy.

Who actually does the work?

One person, and that person is the one who answers your email. There's no account manager, no offshore team and no handoff. That's the reason scope and timelines are fixed rather than open-ended.

Why are there no case studies or client logos?

Because inventing them would be lying, and BlvkWare is new. Instead the three tools in the Lab are the work sample — they're free, they're live, and they do real things. Go use one and judge from that.

Where are you based, and do you work with businesses in other states?

BlvkWare is based in Mississippi and works with businesses across the United States. All of it is done remotely — the systems are software, so there is nothing that needs anyone on site.

In practice that means a call, a written scope, and then the work. Clients in every time zone are fine; the calendar shows real availability in your own time zone when you book.

What kinds of businesses is this for?

Businesses that run on phone calls, quotes and appointments, and lose money every time one of those slips — home services and trades, clinics, salons, law and accounting practices, agencies. The common shape is an owner who knows exactly which job in their business nobody is doing, and no realistic prospect of hiring someone to do it.

The size test is simpler than the industry one. If one average job is worth several hundred dollars to you and you send more quotes or miss more calls in a month than you can personally chase, an agent pays for itself on arithmetic you can check yourself. If your whole month is six jobs, it doesn't, and you should be told that rather than sold to.
